Coastal blue carbon

Purpose

Value the carbon held in the world’s three mapped coastal blue-carbon habitats (mangrove, salt marsh and seagrass) as part of Gross Ecosystem Product. The quantity is mapped habitat area multiplied by a per-habitat carbon density; the price is the same annual rental social cost of carbon that terrestrial carbon storage is valued at (Parisa et al. 2022). The service values storage only. The figure it reports is the carbon standing in the base year, not a flow between years.

Method

Extent

Each habitat comes from its own published extent map, read as polygons:

Habitat Extent
Mangrove Global Mangrove Watch v3, 2019 (Bunting et al. 2018, 2022)
Salt marsh global_salt_marsh2019.gpkg
Seagrass UNEP-WCMC013-014 SeagrassPtPy v7.1 (UNEP-WCMC and Short 2021)

The salt marsh extent is the one input with no published source named in the module, neither the code nor the module bibliography identifies where global_salt_marsh2019.gpkg came from.

Seagrass is optional at the tree level (include_seagrass). A tree built with seagrass but no extent file raises rather than running, so a data gap cannot quietly understate the coastal total; a tree built without seagrass writes every seagrass column at zero, so the combined table has the same shape either way.

Aggregation surface

Coastal habitat is aggregated on the marine surface, eemarine_r566 (one row per exclusive economic zone and coastal region) rather than on the land country surface the other services use. Each habitat layer is clipped to every marine region in turn, and the clipped pieces are measured in the equal-area projection EPSG:6933 (NSIDC EASE-Grid 2.0 Global), which is what turns polygon geometry into hectares.

Salt marsh additionally runs a zonal pass over the hectares-per-cell pyramid, written out as salt_marsh_with_area.gpkg for inspection. The area that reaches the country table is the equal-area geometric area, the same measure the other two habitats use.

Carbon density

Density is resolved per pixel for mangrove and salt marsh, and per polygon for seagrass. Every pool is reported in Mg C per hectare, and soil carbon throughout is the stock in the top 20 cm. The external soil products report the top 1 m, and are taken as a linear share of that profile.

Mangrove. Above-ground biomass follows the Hamilton & Friess latitude regression (Hamilton and Friess 2018), AGB (t/ha) = −6.4305 × |lat| + 271.747, floored at zero (the line crosses zero at roughly 42°, and the floor is what stops it going negative beyond that). Below-ground biomass is AGB times the IPCC zone-specific root-to-shoot ratio (IPCC 2014): 0.49 in the wet tropics, 0.29 in the dry tropics, 0.96 subtropical, with the tropical boundary at |lat| = 23.5° and the wet/dry split at 2000 mm mean annual precipitation. Biomass converts to carbon at 0.48 for AGB (Hamilton and Friess 2018) and 0.39 for BGB (Howard et al. 2014). Soil carbon is read from the Sanderman 30 m mangrove soil map (Sanderman et al. 2018). Where no precipitation raster is supplied the whole tropics take the wet ratio; where no soil raster is supplied, soil falls back to a latitude step function running from 80 Mg C/ha at the equator down to 50 Mg C/ha poleward of 25°.

Salt marsh. Above-ground biomass is the Chmura median of 5 t/ha (Chmura et al. 2003), raised by a factor of 1.2 below |lat| = 25° for tropical productivity. Below-ground biomass is 2.5 times AGB, for the extensive root systems of tidal marsh. Carbon fractions are 0.45 for AGB and 0.41 for BGB. Soil carbon is read from the Maxwell MarSOC map of tidal marsh soils (Maxwell et al. 2024); the fallback step function runs the other way from mangrove, 36 Mg C/ha in the tropics up to 70 Mg C/ha poleward of 45°.

Seagrass. Biomass carbon is genus-specific, from the Gomis global synthesis (Gomis et al. 2025), and is the one density that is an attribute of the polygon rather than of the pixel, the extent map carries a GENUS field. Genera group by life-history strategy: persistent (Posidonia at 9.78 Mg C/ha, the other persistent genera at 3.70), opportunistic (Zostera, Syringodium, Halodule at 0.94) and colonising (Ruppia 0.33, Halophila 0.19). A polygon whose genus is unknown, unreported or absent from the table takes the global mean, 1.55 Mg C/ha. Biomass splits 30% above ground and 70% below. Soil carbon is a constant 22 Mg C/ha, the Fourqurean global mean for the top 20 cm (Fourqurean et al. 2012). Freshwater genera present in the extent map (Trapa, Myriophyllum, Valisneria, Najas and the rest) are zeroed across every pool, soil included, so they contribute nothing to a country’s stock.

Stock

For mangrove and salt marsh, stock is accumulated pixel by pixel on the 10 arc-second grid. Each pixel’s density is evaluated at its latitude, multiplied by the hectares in that cell, and added to the marine region it falls in. A pixel counts only where the habitat mask is set, a region id is present and the cell has area. A no-data pixel inside a soil raster contributes zero soil rather than a missing value, and its biomass still counts. The pass runs in row blocks and skips any block the habitat does not reach.

For seagrass, stock is each clipped polygon’s genus density multiplied by its hectares, summed to the region.

Every habitat therefore reports four columns per region: above-ground, below-ground and soil carbon, and their sum. The total is always the three pools added, never a separately computed figure, so it cannot drift away from the parts it is printed beside.

Price and valuation

The price is the annual rental social cost of carbon for the base year, read from the carbon price series (carbon_prices.xlsx) under the run’s price convention, the same series and convention terrestrial carbon uses, so the two carbon services are priced identically. GEP is then

\[ \text{GEP}_{\text{coastal carbon}} = \text{carbon stock (Mg C)} \times \text{rental SCC (\$ per Mg C)} \]

applied pool by pool, so the written table carries a value for above-ground, below-ground and soil carbon as well as the habitat total. The year and the price used travel in the table beside the values.

Collapse to one row per country

The service produces two tables. The first, gep_by_country_base_year_r566, is the full marine surface: per-habitat area, stock pools and storage values for each of the r566 marine regions, with a matching GeoPackage for mapping. Regions with no coastal habitat at all are dropped from it, they are ocean rows, not zero-value countries.

The second, gep_by_country_base_year, is the country table. Building it takes two steps that each guard against counting the same coast twice:

  1. Keep the _EEZ rows. The marine surface carries a country’s coast more than once, as an EEZ row plus land and territorial rows over the same habitat. Only the _EEZ rows are valued.
  2. Collapse through the canonical r264 row. The r264 correspondence splits large countries into sub-regions (China ×6, India ×6, and four two-way splits), so it is filtered to ee_r264_label == iso3_r250_label before the join. Without that filter the join repeats a split country once per sub-region and the sum multiplies its value. Countries with no coastal habitat report zero rather than dropping out.

Open question: EEZ-only versus all rows

Step 1 above is a choice, not a cleanup. Valuing every r566 row instead of the _EEZ rows returns a strictly larger total, and the gap is exactly the non-EEZ rows, the same coastal habitat counted again under a land or territorial label. The two totals answer different questions. What the module writes is the EEZ-only figure; the all-rows figure is what summing the r566 table gives, and the module does not compute it. The arithmetic of the distinction is pinned in test_eez_only_is_a_strict_subset_of_all_rows.

Sensitivity worth naming

The latitude fallbacks, mangrove soil without the Sanderman raster, salt marsh soil without MarSOC, and the mangrove root ratio without a precipitation raster, change the number a run produces. Which source each run used is written to the log at the point the raster is aligned.

Outputs

Per habitat, in the habitat’s own task directory:

  • <habitat>_area_by_countries2019.{gpkg,csv} (hectares per marine region.
  • <habitat>_carbon_stock_by_countries2019.csv) the four stock pools per marine region (Mg C).
  • <habitat>_storage_value_by_countries2019.csv, the stock pools priced (USD), with the year and the rental SCC used.

Then, across habitats:

  • combined_ecosystem_areas.csv, every habitat’s area and stock on one row per marine region, plus total coastal area and total carbon stock.
  • gep_by_country_base_year_r566.{csv,gpkg} (the marine-surface GEP detail.
  • gep_by_country_base_year.csv) one row per country, the service’s GEP result.
