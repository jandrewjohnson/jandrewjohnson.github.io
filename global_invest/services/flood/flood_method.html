Riverine flood-control ecosystem service

Purpose

Vegetated landscapes intercept rainfall, slow its passage into rivers and store part of it in the soil. Where they do so upstream of towns and farmland, floods downstream are shallower than they would otherwise be, and the damage those floods cause is smaller. That reduction in damage is what this account values.

The scope is river flooding only. Coastal flooding, storm surge and pluvial flooding lie outside it, following the hazard product the account uses. Coverage is 250 countries at 537 m, reported in USD 2019.

Method

Valuing this service requires answering a question that cannot be observed: how much flood damage would occur if landscapes stopped absorbing rain. The account simulates a counterfactual world in which ecosystems no longer retain water, routes the same storms through it, and takes the difference.

Everything turns on one number per location, the depth amplification: the ratio of flood depth in the degraded world to flood depth in the current one. Hydrology has no standard name for it, because it has not previously been needed – it exists here only because there is a paired counterfactual world to run.

Step 1, how absorbent each piece of land is

A curve number between 30 and 100 is assigned to every cell by crossing land cover with soil hydrologic group, from USDA field measurements:

\[S = 25400 / \text{CN} - 254\]

\(S\) is the potential maximum retention in millimetres. Forest on loam holds 208 mm; the same soil stripped bare holds 41 mm. This is the only point in the account where vegetation enters the physics.

Step 2, how much extra water runs off

\[I_a = 0.2S, \qquad Q = (P - I_a)^2 / (P - I_a + S) \text{ for } P > I_a\]

The relation saturates. As \(P\) grows large relative to \(S\), runoff approaches total rainfall regardless of land cover, so the service is concentrated in frequent events and the largest proportional effects appear where storms are small relative to what the soil can hold.

Step 3, following the water downhill

\[\text{ratio}(i) = \text{accum}(Q_\text{degraded})(i) / \text{accum}(Q_\text{current})(i)\]

Routing uses MERIT Hydro. On the Upper Mississippi, seven per cent more runoff at the point of rainfall becomes twenty-two per cent more discharge in the river: routing roughly triples the local effect, and that is what makes flood regulation a landscape service rather than a local one.

Step 4, turning more water into deeper water

\[A(i) = [\text{ratio}(i)]^{f}, \qquad f \approx 0.4\]

At-a-station hydraulic geometry gives the split between width, depth and velocity. So 21.5% more discharge produces 8% more depth. The exponent is the least constrained element of the chain and is swept over 0.3 to 0.5.

Two counterfactuals

Bare soil removes land cover entirely, setting curve numbers to the fallow values of the reference tables. This is the world a gross product asks about, and it is the reference the erosion account uses, so the two services are measured against one hypothetical landscape and can be summed.

In-situ degradation raises curve numbers less: soils compacted and canopy thinned, the land still present. Closer to what realistic degradation would produce, and reported alongside rather than as a sensitivity.

Bare soil exceeds in-situ by about 2.5, and that ratio is stable to within four per cent across both hazard products and both treatments of permanent water.

Lambda

Lambda is 1 for this service. The counterfactual already isolates the ecosystem contribution: it is a difference between two worlds identical in every respect except the ecosystem, so whatever remains in the difference is the ecosystem’s doing by construction.

The prevention share reported throughout is a different quantity and not an attribution coefficient:

\[\text{prevention share} = (\text{EAD}_\text{degraded} - \text{EAD}_\text{current}) / \text{EAD}_\text{degraded}\]

An attribution share near 1 and a prevention share near 0.01 are not two estimates of one thing.

Sign conventions

GEP is positive: it is damage that does not occur. Degradation raises expected annual damage, so the difference is non-negative by construction and a negative value indicates a routing or alignment fault rather than a result.

Compute

The amplification field is built from rainfall and flow direction rather than from the hazard maps, so it survives a change of hazard product. Building it is two weeks of single-threaded flow accumulation over 75 billion cells; everything downstream of it is hours. RUN.md documents both paths.

Before trusting any rerun of the pipeline

The account as it stands does not rerun the pipeline: gep_calculation reads the author’s own per-country export, so the number here is his verified one. This section matters only if that changes.

The failure that made this account wrong twice

The author’s RUN.md is explicit, and it is the most important thing in it:

Check flow-direction coverage. This is not optional, and it is the reason this account was wrong twice.

Seven MERIT flow-direction packages were missing at various points, and every absence was silent. Accumulation over a missing network writes nodata, the amplification clips to a minimum of 1.0, and the valuation reads 1.0 as no change here rather than no data here. Japan, Korea, Portugal and Kazakhstan returned zero service against 49 bn USD of exposure; Nigeria, Chad, Cameroon, Niger and the Central African Republic another 4 bn. Nothing in any log recorded a problem. Both were found because a country looked implausible. Recovering them raised GEP by 18.5 per cent and then a further 16.

python prep_merit_dir.py --check-tiles

⚠ The check has a limit the author states plainly. Its reference list was generated from a tile set believed complete, so it detects regressions — a lost file, a partial copy, an interrupted extraction — but not a package that was never downloaded, which was the original failure.

A missing amplification raster behaves the same way: the pipeline warns, falls back to current depths and returns a GEP of zero, so the job completes and the number is wrong. RUN.md carries a three-line check that the mosaic resolves, and msi/final_run.sbatch verifies the totals itself and prints ALL MATCH or the quantity that differs.

The account should compute this itself

⚠ Reading the author’s per-country export is an interim, not the destination.

Not because every service computes its own — about a third do not, and for some that is right: the account does not recompute the World Bank’s CWON resource rents for extractive materials or energy, because the published rent is the source. The distinction is whether we hold a model we could run.

Flood’s peers are stormwater and air filtration, both recorded as “not yet”: a model that exists, that we have, and that has not been run here. Erosion is the case to follow — it has no scenario shock either, and it still runs the sediment model here and reports $13.96bn against the author’s $14.59bn.

The distinction worth keeping: producing our own number is what the library is for, and it is what makes the figure reproducible, auditable and rerunnable here. Comparing it to the author’s is a fidelity check and not a correctness one. The second does not argue against the first.

Until then, gep_calculation reads flood_gep_for_merge_v2_2024hazard.csv and the account carries his $11.40bn. That is honest and it is not finished.

What running it needs

The inputs are on MSI at /projects/standard/jajohns/shared/flood_gep, read-only, so a run needs a writable root with those inputs linked in and the Sections B and C rasters copied across — about 1 GB, without which a run rebuilds them and takes eleven hours rather than three. In this library the root is flood_root_dir in es_parameters.csv rather than the source repo’s GEP_FLOOD_ROOT environment variable, so only the linking and copying carry over.

⚠ Not done as of 2026-08-28. Nothing here has been run; the account reads his export.
